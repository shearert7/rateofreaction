If your experimental refinement measured mass loss (via mass balance displacement) instead of collecting gas volume, perform this preparatory step for every recorded time interval before calculating mean values:

Mass Lost (g) = Initial Total Mass (g) - Recorded Mass at Time t (g)

2. Background & Calculation Foundations

Use the following formulas and mathematical methods to process your experimental trials:

Calculation Formula Example Calculation
1. Mean (Average)
Mean = Trial 1 + Trial 2 + Trial 3 3
If trial volumes at t = 30s are 112 mL, 124 mL, and 133 mL:

Mean = 112 + 124 + 133 3 = 123.0 mL
2. Rate of Reaction
Rate = Gas Produced (mL or g) Time (s)
If 123.0 mL of gas is produced over 30 seconds:

Rate = 123.0 30 = 4.10 mL/s
3. Absolute Error
Absolute Error = Max Value - Min Value 2
For trials with values 112 mL, 124 mL, and 133 mL:

Absolute Error = 133 - 112 2 = 10.5 mL
4. Percentage Error
% Error = Absolute Error Mean × 100
Using Absolute Error = 10.5 mL and Mean = 123.0 mL:

% Error = 10.5 123.0 × 100 = 8.54%

3. Presenting Processed Data in Your Report

In your scientific report, all processed data must be clearly presented in the Processed Data section. Ensure you include the following elements:

  • Tables of Processed Data: Present structured summary tables that detail the average CO₂ produced over time, calculated rate of reaction, and both absolute and percentage uncertainties for each experimental condition.
  • Sample Calculations: Include one clear, step-by-step example for each calculation type (Mean, Rate of Reaction, Absolute Error, and Percentage Error) showing the general formula, substituted numbers, and final units.
  • Graphical Representation: Include a fully labeled graph showing CO₂ produced over time (or mass lost over time) across your tested conditions, including trendlines and appropriate error bars where applicable.
