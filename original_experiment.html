Mandatory Practical: Surface Area and Reaction Rate

Aim

Conduct an experiment to test the effect of increasing surface area on the rate of a reaction between calcium carbonate and hydrochloric acid.

Theoretical Background: Surface Area & Collision Theory
According to collision theory, for reactants to react they must collide with each other in the correct orientation and with enough energy (greater than or equal to the activation energy).

Increasing the surface area of a solid reactant means that more reactant particles are exposed to the surrounding liquid or gas. Breaking a solid into smaller pieces (or using a powder instead of large chips) increases the total number of exposed surface particles available to collide. This higher exposure leads to more frequent successful collisions per unit of time, which increases the overall rate of the chemical reaction.

Materials & Apparatus

  • Setup Apparatus: Stand and clamp, water trough, 100 mL measuring cylinder, 100 mL conical flask with stopper and plastic tubing.
  • Reactants: 0.5 g calcium carbonate powder, 0.5 g calcium carbonate chips, 25 mL of 0.5 M hydrochloric acid.
  • Timing & Measurement: Stopwatch and volumetric gas measuring setup.

Experimental Variables

Variable Type Description
Independent Variable Surface area of calcium carbonate — Powder (large surface area) vs. Chips/Rocks (smaller surface area).
Dependent Variable Volume of carbon dioxide gas produced (mL) recorded every 30 seconds.
Controlled Variables Mass of calcium carbonate (0.5 g), volume of acid (25 mL), acid concentration (0.5 M), temperature, and atmospheric pressure.

Methodology

  1. Set up the conical flask, stopper, tubing, water trough, measuring cylinder, clamp, and stand according to Diagram 1.
  2. Weigh out 0.5 g of calcium carbonate powder and place it into the conical flask.
  3. Measure out 25 mL of 0.5 M hydrochloric acid and set it aside.
  4. Prepare a stopwatch and assign a designated reader to observe the measuring cylinder volume every 30 seconds.
  5. Pour the prepared acid into the conical flask and immediately seal it securely with the rubber stopper.
  6. Record the volume of carbon dioxide gas produced at 30-second intervals.
  7. Stop recording when the gas volume remains unchanged for more than 2 minutes, or when 5 minutes (300 s) have elapsed.
  8. Empty, rinse, and clean the conical flask.
  9. Repeat the procedure using 0.5 g of calcium carbonate chips (rocks).

Limitations vs. Uncertainty

Limitations are flaws or constraints in the experimental design or methodology itself (e.g., small sample size, single trial, or uncontrolled variablesFactors that were not kept constant during the experiment, which might secretly affect the results.).
Uncertainty is the quantifiable doubt in a measured numerical value based on equipment limits (e.g., ±0.05 g or ±0.5 mL).

Background

In a student experiment, students modify (i.e. refine, extend or redirect) an experiment in order to address their own related hypothesis or question.

Cognition: modify — change the form or qualities of; make partial or minor changes to something

Modifier Definition Instruction Example
Refine “to make improvement by introducing subtleties or distinctions” • make improvements to the investigation without changing the independent or dependent variables
• increasing the sample size
• using more precise equipment (data logging equipment)
• make improvements to the methodology
Extend “to increase the scope, meaning, or application of” • change the range of independent variable points tested
• collect additional dependent variables
• use a different experimental model

Examples: test different range of temperature points; collect more than one dependent variable i.e. mass as well as height; extend the scope by using different model organisms (i.e. different species)
Redirect “to change the course or direction of” • altering the independent variable

Examples: testing pH instead of temperature; testing different solutions; testing solution concentrationHow much solute is dissolved in a liquid (e.g. molarity). instead of time
